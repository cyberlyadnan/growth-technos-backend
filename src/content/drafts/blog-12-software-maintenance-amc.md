Software is not a one-time purchase. Your website or business app keeps running on servers, frameworks and plugins that change all the time. Security updates come out, payment gateways change their rules, browsers update, and someone has to keep it all working.

That is what a maintenance plan or AMC (annual maintenance contract) is for. This guide explains what should be included, what it costs, and the warning signs to watch for.

## What a good maintenance plan includes

**Bug fixes.** Things that worked and stopped working. A good plan says how quickly they will be fixed.

**Security updates.** Updating the framework, libraries, plugins and server software when security issues are found. This is the part most people skip until they get hacked.

**Backups.** Regular automatic backups of the database and files, stored separately, and tested so you know they actually restore.

**Uptime monitoring.** Someone gets an alert when your site or app goes down, instead of finding out from a customer.

**Renewals.** Domain, SSL certificate and hosting renewals tracked, so your site doesn't go offline because a card expired.

**Small changes.** A fixed number of hours each month for small updates like text changes, a new field in a form, or a small report tweak.

**Performance checks.** Keeping page speed and server performance under control as data grows.

## What maintenance usually does not include

- New features or modules (these are quoted separately)
- Complete design overhauls or rebranding
- Problems caused by third parties modifying backend files without coordination
- Direct infrastructure costs (cloud hosting, paid SaaS API tokens, payment gateway charges)

Get this list in writing upfront so expectations remain transparent and aligned.

## What it costs in India

Maintenance pricing depends on complexity, transaction volume, and operational criticality:

- **Simple marketing websites:** Usually ₹2,000 to ₹3,500/month covering weekly security patches, cloud backups, and minor content updates.
- **E-commerce sites and Web Apps:** ₹4,000 to ₹8,000/month because checkout pipelines, database locks, and payment webhooks require active supervision.
- **Mission-critical business software (ERP, CRM, Inventory):** ₹7,500 to ₹15,000+/month with guaranteed SLA response times under 2 hours and dedicated staging environments.

## Warning signs with your current developer

- They take days or weeks to reply when critical checkout or invoice workflows break.
- You do not possess root cloud hosting access, domain credentials, or repository ownership.
- The underlying framework and libraries have remained unpatched for years.
- Backups exist "somewhere in the cloud", but nobody has ever executed a verified restore drill.
- Every minor label tweak generates a brand new invoice and a two-week delay.

If two or more of these apply to your organization, your technical infrastructure is operating at extreme risk.

## How to choose a maintenance plan

Before signing an AMC with any software development partner, verify these core criteria:
- **Defined SLAs:** What is the contractual response window for high-severity outages vs standard requests?
- **Rollover hours:** How many minor modification hours are bundled each month, and do unused hours roll over?
- **Disaster recovery drills:** How frequently are backup archives test-restored to verify database integrity?
- **Code ownership:** If you choose to terminate the contract, do you retain complete, unencumbered ownership of all code assets?

### FAQ

**What is AMC for a website?**
An annual maintenance contract covering updates, security, backups, bug fixes and small changes for a fixed monthly or yearly fee.

**How much does website maintenance cost in India?**
It depends on the type of site. Our plans start at ₹3,000 a month, with higher plans for e-commerce and business software.

**Can you maintain software built by another developer?**
Yes. We start with a handover and code review, take full backups, fix urgent issues, and then move to regular maintenance.

**Do I need maintenance if my website is working fine?**
Yes. Most problems, especially security issues, build up quietly. Regular updates and backups prevent expensive emergencies later.
