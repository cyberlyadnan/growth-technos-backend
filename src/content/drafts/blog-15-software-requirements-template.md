Here is something that surprises a lot of business owners. Send the same vague idea to five software companies and you will get five quotes ranging from ₹20,000 to ₹5 lakh.

They aren't all cheating you. They are each imagining a different product, because the brief left room for guesswork.

The fix is a clear requirements document. You don't need technical jargon for this. You need to describe your business and your problem clearly. This template shows you how.

## Why this matters

A clear project brief gets you:
- Quotes you can actually compare on an apples-to-apples basis.
- Fewer arguments later about what was included in scope.
- A faster start, because the developer understands your business from day one.

## The 11-step requirements template

Copy these 11 headings into a document and answer each one in plain words. Short bullet points are fine.

1. **About your business:** Two or three lines. What you sell, to whom, how many staff, how many locations.
2. **The problem today:** What is going wrong? Where do you lose time or money? Be specific. "Dealers send orders on WhatsApp and we miss some" is far more useful than "we need better order management".
3. **Who will use the software:** List the roles and roughly how many people in each. For example: owner (1), office staff (3), salesmen (8), dealers (60).
4. **The main workflows, step by step:** Describe how work flows today, from start to finish. For example, how an order moves from enquiry to dispatch to payment.
5. **Must-have vs nice-to-have:** Split your feature list into two columns. Version one should contain only the must-haves.
6. **Reports you want:** Which three or four reports would you open every week? Name them.
7. **Integrations:** Tally, WhatsApp, payment gateway, courier partner, an existing website or app?
8. **Data you have now:** Excel sheets, old software, registers? Roughly how many customers, products and past records?
9. **Devices:** Will people use it on a computer, a phone or both? Do any users work in areas with poor network?
10. **Budget range and timeline:** A range is fine. It helps the developer suggest what fits, instead of guessing.
11. **Who decides:** Who approves the design, tests the software and signs off each milestone?

## A short practical example

Here is how a small distributor might fill in a few sections:
- **Problem today:** *"60 dealers order by WhatsApp and phone. Rates are different for each dealer. We sometimes bill the wrong rate and miss orders sent late at night."*
- **Users:** *"Owner (1), 3 office staff, 8 salesmen, 60 dealers."*
- **Must-have:** *"Dealers order from their phone with their own price list. Office sees all orders in one place. Stock is checked before confirming."*
- **Nice-to-have:** *"Salesman visit tracking, scheme management."*
- **Integration:** *"Invoices should go into Tally."*

That is already enough for an experienced engineering team to give you an accurate, fixed quote.

## Tips that save time and money

- **Voice notes are fine:** If writing is hard, record a 10-minute voice note explaining your process. A good developer will turn it into a document.
- **Share screenshots of your Excel sheets:** They show your real columns and calculations better than any description.
- **List the exceptions:** "Usually X happens, but sometimes Y." The exceptions are where custom software earns its money.
- **Don't design the screens yourself:** Describe the business problem and let the UI/UX team engineer the solution.

### FAQ

**Do I need technical knowledge to write software requirements?**
No. Describe your business, your problem and how work flows today. The developer handles the technical side.

**How long should a requirements document be?**
For most small business software, two to four pages is enough.

**What is an SRS document?**
A Software Requirements Specification is a detailed technical version of your brief. For small projects, the developer usually prepares it from your simple brief.

**Can requirements change after the project starts?**
Small changes are normal. Agree upfront how changes will be priced so there are no surprises.
