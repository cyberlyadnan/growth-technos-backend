A customer calls: "Where is my order?" The office calls the supervisor. The supervisor walks the floor, checks the paper job card, asks two operators, and calls back 20 minutes later with "it should be ready by Friday".

If this happens in your unit every day, you don't have a production problem. You have a visibility problem. Production tracking software fixes that.

## What goes wrong without tracking

- Nobody knows the exact status of an order without walking the floor.
- Paper job cards get lost, smudged or filled in at the end of the day from memory.
- Material issued to a job isn't recorded, so you never know the real material cost per order.
- Rejections and rework aren't tracked, so the same problems keep happening.
- Delays are noticed too late, usually when the customer calls.
- The owner has no daily picture of what was produced and what is stuck. Check out our guide on [7 signs your business has outgrown Excel](/blog/signs-business-outgrown-excel).

## What production tracking software should do

**Digital job cards.** Every order becomes a job card with its stages, for example cutting, machining, plating, inspection, packing and dispatch. Your stages, in your order.

**Updates from the shop floor.** Operators or supervisors update the stage from a phone or a shared tablet. Even better, scan a QR code on the job card at each stage. One tap, no typing.

**Material issue against each job.** Raw material issued from the store is recorded against the job, so you see actual consumption and cost.

**Rejection and rework recording.** Quantity rejected, at which stage, and why. After a few months, this data shows you exactly where quality problems come from.

**Machine and operator assignment.** Which job is on which machine, with which operator.

**Delay alerts.** If a job hasn't moved from a stage within the expected time, the supervisor gets an alert.

**Dispatch linked to production.** Finished jobs move to packing and dispatch, with challans and invoices generated from the same record.

**An owner's dashboard.** Today's production, jobs running late, rejection rate, and orders ready to dispatch, on your phone. See our [ERP software cost guide](/blog/erp-software-cost-small-business-india) for comprehensive scoping.

## Making it work on the shop floor

Most shop floor software fails for one reason: it is built for the office, not the floor. Operators wearing gloves, standing at a machine, will not type long entries.

What works:
- Big buttons and very few fields.
- Hindi and regional language labels where needed.
- QR or barcode scanning instead of manual typing.
- One shared rugged tablet per section if not everyone has a smartphone.
- A supervisor who verifies updates during the initial transition period.

## Start small

You don't need full production planning on day one. Start with stage tracking only. Once every job's status is visible, add material issue, then rejections, then planning. Each step is useful on its own.

### FAQ

**Do operators need smartphones?**
Not necessarily. A shared tablet per section, with QR scanning, works well in many units.

**Can production tracking work with Tally?**
Yes. Production and dispatch data can connect with billing, and invoices can sync with Tally.

**Is this the same as an ERP?**
It is one part of an ERP. Many small manufacturers start with production tracking and add inventory, purchase and billing later.

**How long does it take to set up?**
A focused stage-tracking system usually takes a few weeks, including setting up your stages and training supervisors.
