Real estate businesses spend serious money on leads. Portal subscriptions, Facebook ads, Google ads, hoardings, channel partners. Then a large share of those leads gets a late call, one follow-up, and nothing after that.

In property, the sale often takes weeks or months. A buyer who isn't ready today may book in three months, if someone is still following up. That is what a good real estate CRM is for.

## Where real estate leads get lost

- **Leads arrive from too many places:** 99acres, MagicBricks, Housing.com, Facebook and Instagram ads, the website, walk-ins and phone calls. Each lands in a different inbox.
- **Slow first call:** A buyer enquires on three projects at once. The first developer to call usually gets the site visit.
- **Site visits aren't tracked:** Who visited, who brought them, what they liked, and what happened after.
- **Channel partner disputes:** Two brokers claim the same buyer, and nobody has a clear record of who registered them first.
- **Unit availability lives in Excel:** Sales staff quote a flat that was booked yesterday.

## Features a real estate CRM should have

**Lead capture from every source.** Portal leads, ad leads, website forms and manual entry for calls and walk-ins, all in one list. Most portals send leads by email or offer an integration, and both can be pulled in automatically.

**Fast assignment and first-call alerts.** New leads go to a salesperson within minutes, with a reminder if nobody has called within a set time.

**Lead stages built for property.** New, contacted, site visit scheduled, site visit done, negotiation, booked, lost. With a lost reason every time.

**Site visit management.** Schedule visits, send WhatsApp confirmations and reminders, record feedback after the visit.

**Inventory view.** Tower, floor and unit-wise status: available, on hold, booked, sold. Sales staff see live availability instead of asking the office.

**Channel partner management.** Brokers register their clients in the system with a timestamp, see their leads' status, and track commission. This alone prevents many disputes.

**Booking and payment tracking.** Booking amount, agreement, and the payment schedule, whether construction-linked or time-linked, with reminders for upcoming instalments.

**Reports for management.** Leads and cost per source, site visits, conversions by salesperson, and which projects or unit types are moving.

## A note on RERA

RERA rules require registered projects to show their registration details in advertising. Make sure your website, landing pages and any automated messages include the correct RERA number for each project. A CRM can store this per project so it is never missed.

## Ready-made or custom?

Ready-made real estate CRMs are a reasonable start for a small brokerage with a few agents.

Custom makes sense for builders and larger firms that need:

- live unit inventory linked to bookings,
- channel partner portals with their own login,
- payment schedules matching their actual plans,
- a buyer app or portal showing payment status and construction updates,
- no per-user fees as the sales team grows.

### FAQ

**Can a CRM pull leads from 99acres and MagicBricks automatically?**
Usually yes. Portals send leads by email or through integrations, and these can be captured into the CRM automatically.

**What is the best CRM for real estate builders in India?**
Small brokerages can start with ready-made tools. Builders with live inventory, channel partners and payment schedules usually need a custom or heavily customised system.

**Can channel partners use the CRM?**
Yes. A partner portal lets brokers register clients, track status and see commission, which reduces disputes.

**How much does a real estate CRM cost?**
A focused lead CRM starts at ₹30,000 with us. Full systems with inventory, partner portals and apps cost more depending on scope.
